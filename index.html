I am a research assistant in the Systems and Artificial Intelligence (SAI) Lab at New York University, working with Prof. Sai Qian Zhang. I study how to make large language models cheaper and faster to run: post-training quantization at very low bit widths, and efficient serving across many machines with speculative decoding.

I received my M.S. in Computer Engineering from NYU in 2026 and my B.Sc. in Data Science and Technology from HKUST in 2024. Before focusing on LLM efficiency, I worked on deep learning for cancer immunotherapy screening with Prof. Weiqiang Chen at NYU, and on hand-tracking interaction in virtual reality with Prof. Xiaojuan Ma at HKUST.

I am applying to Ph.D. programs for Fall 2027.

Publications

* Equal contribution

CoBIT: Unified Optimization of Codebooks, Assignments, and Activation Composition for Extreme Low-Bit LLM Quantization

Xiangyang Yin, Feiyu Jia, Henry Kam, Tianhua Xia, Qi Sun, Sai Qian Zhang

Under reviewICLR 2027

Post-training quantization that jointly optimizes Hessian-aware weight codebooks, error-compensated assignments, and a per-token FP4 exponent–mantissa format, with a fused CUDA kernel for inference.

  • +35.9 pts vs. best baseline, A4W2
  • +4.8 pts vs. best baseline, A4W3
  • up to 1.53× faster decoding than FP16, A4W4

OpenReviewCode upon acceptance

DSD-Sim: A Simulation Framework for Distributed Speculative Decoding in Multi-Node LLM Serving

Leshu Li, Fengze Yu, Yifei Feng, Feiyu Jia, Jian Li, Hao Wang, Bradley McDanel, Sai Qian Zhang

Under reviewAAAI 2027

A discrete-event simulator for speculative decoding across draft and target node pools that models routing, batching, speculation windows and network topology, calibrated against real GPUs and NS-3.

  • 3.2% decode-latency error vs. real GPUs
  • ≈3% network error vs. NS-3
  • 4,096 nodes simulated

Video Face Swap with DeepFaceLab

Feiyu Jia*, Sihan Yang*

PublishedProc. SPIE 12168 (ICCAID 2021), 2022

Face swapping in video with a SAEHD autoencoder and S3FD scale-invariant face detection. Co-first and corresponding author.

Projects

  1. Feb 2026

    EcoTraining: energy-efficient gradient compression for LLM training

    Cognichip Hackathon · team of four · GitHub

    With three teammates, I designed SystemVerilog hardware in which large gradients go straight to memory while small updates accumulate in an on-chip SRAM cache until they cross a threshold. It cut DRAM writes by 52% in testbench simulation.
